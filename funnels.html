How the City went about identifying pieds-à-terre, and what it should have done instead

The surcharge applies only to homes that are not the owner's primary residence. Switch between the Department of Finance's actual process and the process recommended in the accompanying essay.

How the City narrowed the list

Finance published the entire property roll under the banner of the surcharge, then mailed about 17,000 letters saying the property "may be subject" to the tax. None says why it arrived.

Step 1 · What Finance published

959,710

Properties on the full roll

684,619 houses · 230,885 condos · 44,206 co-op units

Essentially every home in the city, released under the banner of a tax almost none of them owe.

Step 2 · Over the value threshold

31,558

Houses at $5M or more; units at $1M or more

6,802 houses · 11,830 condos · 12,926 co-op units

Nearly every co-op building in the city — 6,590 of 6,732 — holds at least one unit over the line.

Step 3 · The published subject list

24,218

Properties Finance named

6,802 houses · 11,830 condos · 5,586 co-op units

Finance listed 5,586 co-op units, not the 12,926 actually at $1 million or more. That undercount is the "24,000 versus 31,000" gap.

Step 4 · The burden shifts

≈17,000

Letters mailed · burden of proof: the owner

The letter names no missing document and gives no reason it arrived — including to owners Finance had already accepted as residents.

What it produced

Thousands of letters went to owners Finance had already screened out, the co-op count was published wrong, and the public roll was broad enough to include the finance commissioner's own home and a former mayor's — both well under the threshold.

How the City could narrow the list

Start from the complete value screen, then run two screens on data Finance already holds or can lawfully obtain. Only the genuinely ambiguous cases get a letter — and that letter says why.

Start here · The full value screen

31,558

Every property over the threshold

6,802 houses · 11,830 condos · 12,926 co-op units

Publish this list, complete and unit-level, rather than the full roll — with a plain caveat that clearing the value line is not the same as owing the tax.

Screen one · What Finance already knows

Clear its own records first

The yearly owner-occupancy certification behind the co-op/condo abatement, plus the senior, veteran and STAR exemptions — each already required proof of primary residence.

Screen two · What the State can confirm

Match the State return

The surcharge test is the address on the New York State income tax return. Finance doesn't hold it, the State does, and the statute lets the two departments share it.

Screen three · Who is actually left

5,374

Properties that plausibly remain subject

1,709 houses · 3,104 condos · 561 co-op units

$450.1 million

Model estimate

These are the LLCs, the trusts, the rentals and the family-occupied homes — and Finance knows which signal was missing for each, so the letter can name it and say exactly what to send.

What it would produce

About 26,000 of the 31,558 — roughly five in six — clear without the owner sending anything. The 5,374 that remain carry an estimated $450.1 million, close to the roughly $500 million the City projected for this year.

Figures and how to read them

Steps 1 through 3 are counted from the Department of Finance supplemental rolls — TC1 for Class 1, TC2 for Class 2, both 2027. The full roll totals 959,710 properties, reported publicly as "more than 900,000." The value thresholds are inclusive: the statute covers property valued equal to or greater than $5 million for houses and $1 million for condo and co-op units.

Finance's July 24 subject list published 5,586 co-op units rather than the 12,926 units actually valued at $1 million or more. That undercount accounts for almost all of the difference between 31,558 and 24,218 — the "24,000 versus 31,000" discrepancy that appeared in press coverage.

The roughly 17,000 letters mailed is as reported. The final stage — 5,374 properties and $450.1 million — comes from a value-band liability model, not from an agency count. It estimates what plausibly stays subject after Finance's own exemptions and abatement, individually owned primary residences the State could confirm, and the other proof pathways; it treats a house held in the owner's own name as a strong primary-residence signal that largely clears. The roughly 26,000 cleared is 31,558 minus 5,374.

Band widths are illustrative, not drawn to scale. A true-to-scale funnel starting at 959,710 would render every later stage invisible.
